How it is graded

Carat

Carat = Cut × Color × Clarity. Cut is our hit chance minus the book's, in points. Color is the odds. Clarity shrinks the stone when the read is shaky. Under 5 is dirt, below 0 is fool's gold. Carat works out to roughly how far the price is off, in percent.

Cut v3 · real stats

Our chance starts from nflverse play by play: carries and targets inside the 10 yard line times the league touchdown rate on each, plus long touchdowns scaled to snap share, recalibrated on 1,367 player games from weeks 4 to 8 (forecast error 0.131 against 0.137 for plain hit rates). That stats read is then blended with the price after removing the house cut, which grows on long shots. Long shots get less pull away from the market. Players with no 2026 stats get no read.

Clarity v3

A 100 point score. Sample counts touchdowns we would expect to have seen in his real games. Consistency checks that red zone usage and actual touchdowns tell the same story. Books agreeing adds up to 15, and setup uses the official injury report plus a falling snap share. Clear 80+ ×1.0, slight haze 65+ ×0.85, hazy 50+ ×0.7, cloudy ×0.55.

Freeze and close

Prices are captured before anyone knows the result, and never changed after. The last capture before kickoff is the close. Line value is our price over the closing price, minus 1: positive means the market moved our way, the best early sign the reads are real.

Linked groups

Bets on the same player and the same event count once for hit rate, expected hits and forecast error, using the biggest stone in the group. Profit still adds every leg.

Honest read

One graded Sunday is noise. A tier's hit rate starts to mean something after roughly 100 graded props in it, which the whole board reaches in about two weeks. Board grades are mechanical: the researched picks layer news and matchup on top.
